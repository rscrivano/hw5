3 · What if one agent had all the tools?

Routing would be easier. There'd be no delegate calls, none of the 5 refused hand-offs from the first passes, and no misrouted Accounting call on 102.

But the energy spent would be higher and less efficient:

  • Every call would carry all 29 tool descriptions and every role's rules; today each agent sees 5–11. With input already ~95% of the cost, a bigger context on every call costs more on every one of the 125 calls in this run.
  • One context would have to hold price, stock, vendor, rent and customer tone at once. On 103, the hardest ticket, that context would keep growing over three passes instead of being split into short specialist runs.
  • Separation of duties would disappear. Facilities can only request rent, Accounting only invoices and POs, and Customer Service can't touch money. Those boundaries caught the duplicate rent request on 102 and the duplicate invoice request on 103.

The honest counterpoint from this run: specialization has overhead (102 pass 1, 101 pass 2). The fix was not one agent but tighter routing, and 102 pass 2 got the specialist answer at single-agent cost.

Takeaways

  • The safety design held: $3,272 paid, every dollar human-approved, the unaffordable $264 request rejected, and the books tie out at $128.00.
  • The routing was right from the start: Boss's first call matched the plan on every first pass. Discipline after the first call is where tokens leaked. The fixes (ticket facts in each hand-off, one hand-off for single-area tickets, Customer Service last, a PO cash guard) cut the later passes to 12k–27k tokens.
  • Next improvement: resume a ticket automatically when a human approves, so follow-ups don't need a manual re-run.
